The core idea

A license is not a sale.

The difference decides everything about how this works — who keeps control, who can say no, and how often the value can be earned.

A sale

Ownership transfers to the buyer. You lose the asset, the control, and every future use of it. The value is paid once — if it is priced right at all.

A license

Ownership never moves. A buyer receives defined rights — for defined uses, for a defined term — and returns or destroys the data when the term ends. The same data can be licensed again, to the next buyer.

Why IEC only licenses

Every agreement in the Network is a license. Your data remains your asset on your balance sheet, under your control — IEC's mandate is to license it well, never to own it.

The process

Five steps, from counts to license.

01

Questionnaire — counts only

You complete a short online evaluation for your segment: volumes, years of history, system names. Numbers and descriptions only — never records, never personal information. It costs nothing and commits you to nothing.

02

Exclusive data grant

You sign an exclusive data licensing grant to IEC. That signature is what makes your data licensable inventory: IEC becomes the sole counterparty to buyers, and your approval rights are written into the grant itself.

03

Buyers evaluate and quote

Vetted end buyers evaluate the counts and quote real numbers. They see the inventory at network level — composition and volumes, never your name. You are never identified to a buyer.

04

De-identification, inside your systems

Before anything moves, data is de-identified to the HIPAA standard — Expert Determination — inside your own systems, under business associate agreements. Zero identifiable information ever leaves.

05

You decide

Terms are presented to you before anything is final. Only then is the license signed — by IEC in its own name — and your statements show exactly what was licensed, to whom, and for what.

Your rights

Rights you keep — all of them.

Ownership

Your data stays yours — during the grant, during every license, and after. Nothing in any Network agreement transfers ownership to IEC or to a buyer.

Approve buyer categories

You approve the categories of buyers your data may be offered to. Categories you have not approved are never approached with your data in the pool.

Exclude any buyer by name

Name a buyer and they are out — no explanation needed. Your exclusion list is written into your grant and honored on every transaction.

Approve every sample

No sample of your data is shown to any buyer until an NDA is executed, de-identification is complete, and you have approved that sample. No exceptions.

Statements & audit rights

Every license produces a statement: what was licensed, to whom, for what purpose, and what you were paid — backed by your right to audit.

The final word

A quote is an offer, not an obligation. If the terms are not right, you decline — and nothing proceeds. Your signature on the grant starts evaluations; it never pre-approves a deal.

Protections

Before any data moves.

Counts first, always

Evaluation runs on counts, date ranges, and system names. Patient information, customer lists, and personal data are never requested and should never be sent.

NDA before any detail

No detail about the Network's inventory is shared with anyone before a mutual confidentiality agreement is in place — and even then, only at network level.

De-identified, in your systems

De-identification happens inside your own environment, under BAA, to the Expert Determination standard. Your systems stay yours — IEC MSO provides management services, not software you must adopt.

Covenants on every license

Every buyer signs up to the same discipline: defined use only, no re-identification, no onward transfer, security obligations, and return or certified destruction at the end of the term.

  • Zero PHI, by design. Counts for evaluation; de-identified data for delivery. There is no step at which identifiable information is needed.
  • Direct buyers only. The Network engages end buyers who sign and pay as principals — no brokers, no marketplaces, no intermediaries.
  • One counterparty. You deal with IEC, and only IEC. Buyers contract with IEC in its own name.
The price

What sets the number.

Buyers price three things: volume — how much data an operation produces; years of history — how deep the stored record runs; and standardization — the same workflows repeated across locations, which trains better models. Approved data partnerships in this market are published at $100K–$2M per partnership, and larger, longer-running, multi-location operations price at the top of that range.

Every quote is a single all-in license value for the transaction — one number, quoted to the buyer, against which your grant's terms apply. The economics of your grant are written into it and approved by you before anything is marketed; your statements then show each transaction in full.
