About data licensing

What your data is worth — and how licensing works.

Every operating business generates data: records, workflows, schedules, transactions, billing patterns. AI companies train their systems on exactly this real-world material — and they pay to license it. Licensing is how that value reaches the owner without the owner ever giving the data up.

A license, never a sale

Ownership never transfers. A buyer receives defined rights, for defined uses, for a defined term — and the same data can be licensed to more than one buyer, so its value can be earned repeatedly, not once.

What drives the price

Three things: volume — how much data an operation produces; years of history — how deep the stored record runs; and standardization — the same workflows repeated across locations. Multi-site operators on shared systems hold the strongest hand, because consistent data trains better models.

The published range

Approved data partnerships in this market are published at $100K–$2M per partnership, priced on the evaluation of volume, history, and standardization. Larger, longer-running, multi-location operations price at the top of that range.

Counts first — nothing identifiable

Evaluation starts from counts only: volumes, date ranges, system names. No patient information, no customer lists, no personal data of any kind. Anything that ever moves to a buyer is de-identified first, under expert certification.

How it works

Five steps, from counts to license.

01

Questionnaire — counts only

The owner completes a short evaluation questionnaire: volumes, years of history, system names. Numbers and descriptions only — never records, never personal information.

02

Exclusive data grant

The owner signs an exclusive data licensing grant to IEC, making IEC the sole counterparty to buyers. Ownership stays with the owner; approval rights over buyer categories are written in.

03

Buyers evaluate and quote

Vetted buyers evaluate the counts and quote against real numbers. The inventory is described at network level — no owner is identified to a buyer.

04

De-identification, inside your systems

Before anything moves, data is de-identified to the HIPAA standard — Expert Determination — inside the owner's own systems, under business associate agreements. Zero identifiable information ever leaves.

05

The owner decides

Terms are presented before anything is final. The license is then signed by IEC in its own name, with statements showing exactly what was licensed, to whom, and for what.

Protections & discipline

Governed at every step.

Direct buyers only

The Network engages end buyers directly. No brokers, no marketplaces, no intermediaries — IEC is the owner's sole counterparty, and buyers contract with IEC in its own name.

Your systems stay yours

Member practices keep their own clinical systems and software. IEC MSO provides management services — the mandate covers data licensing, not tools. Nothing about your operation has to change.

NDA before any detail

No detail is shared before a mutual confidentiality agreement is in place. The inventory is presented at network level only — composition and counts, never an identified owner.

No samples without approval

No sample data is ever shared before an NDA is executed, de-identification is complete, and the owner has approved the sample. Every license carries no-re-identification and no-onward-transfer covenants.

  • Ownership never transfers. Licenses are defined-use and defined-term; the data remains the owner's asset.
  • Full transparency. Statements and audit rights on every license — owners always know what was licensed, to whom, and for what purpose.
  • Zero PHI, by design. Evaluation runs on counts; delivery runs on de-identified data only.
